# Facilitron marketing copy

The brand voice for **published** copy: articles, press releases, toolkit items, guides, case
studies, webinar pages, social, email, onesheets, and video. Pair it with
[tools/voice/facilitron-voice.md](facilitron-voice.md), which carries the rules every piece of copy
follows including internal prose. This file adds what only published work needs.

Read it before drafting, not after. Structure for each format stays in that format's own skill; this
file never restates a dateline template or a component trap.

## Core identity

Facilitron writes like a district business official who has read the actual fee schedule. The stance
is stewardship: public buildings are public assets, and the money and the decisions around them
should be visible. The register is operational and unembarrassed about specifics, because the
audience runs facilities for a living and can tell within two sentences whether the writer has ever
seen a work order.

Not a thought leader. Not a disruptor. A practitioner who brought receipts.

## Where this came from

Derived from the published corpus in `marketing-pages/content/resources` (news, toolkit,
case-studies, webinars), weighted toward 2026 material, which is where the current voice lives. It
describes what Facilitron sounds like. If it stops matching the corpus, the corpus wins and this file
gets re-derived.

## The stance

These are the positions the copy argues. They are load-bearing, and a draft that contradicts
one is off-voice even when every sentence is clean.

- **Community use is a governance question, not an operations question.** The failure mode is not
  that districts are too generous. It is that nobody voted on the generosity.
- **"No subsidization without deliberation."** Subsidies are fine. Undocumented, unauthorized,
  accidental subsidies are not. The house framing: the question isn't whether districts should
  support community use, they should; it's whether those subsidies are visible, intentional, and
  authorized by the board, or happening by default.
- **Access and cost recovery are not in tension.** They reinforce each other. Never argue the
  trade-off version.
- **Never scold a district.** Districts are not the problem; policies written in 1974 and never
  revisited are the problem. Report findings about systems. A named district is always the one that
  fixed something, never the one that got caught.
- **The product is the last paragraph, not the thesis.** Establish the operational problem, walk the
  reader through solving it by hand, then note that districts often do this in Works or DevFees. One
  link, stated as a fact about what districts do.

## The proof set

The recurring, sourced figures. Use these; do not invent new ones and do not round them up.

- More than 15,000 schools across 34 states.
- More than $500 million recovered by districts through community facility-use programs since 2014.
- 8 out of 10 districts assessed had community-user fees that did not cover basic operating costs.
- Most districts assessed had not updated fee schedules in more than seven years.
- Conejo Valley Unified: facility-use revenue grew from $250,000 to more than $800,000 after reform,
  from a baseline where some groups paid about 24 cents an hour.
- The National Model School Board Policy is published CC BY 4.0, free to adopt or adapt.

Quotes are attributed with full name and title on first reference. Executives make arguments;
district leaders describe their own experience. A quote that only praises the platform belongs in a
case study, spoken by the district, not in an article.

## Register by format

Person and formality are set by the format, not by preference. In the toolkit corpus "you/your"
appears constantly and "we/our" appears zero times; in news it is third person throughout.

| Format | Person | Register | CTA | Never |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Press release | third | wire-formal, inverted pyramid | none; media contacts close it | first person, product pitch, adjectives in the lead |
| News article | third | journalistic, finding first | one, late | opinion in Facilitron's voice without a named speaker |
| Toolkit item | second | imperative, procedural | one, last | "we", concept explainers, uncompletable steps |
| Guide | second | instructional, longer breath | one, last | scolding, assumed ignorance |
| Case study | third about the district | narrative, district is protagonist | one, last | Facilitron as the hero of its own case study |
| Webinar page | second, plus "in this session, we cover" | practical, audience named by job title | one, watch/register | hype about the session |
| Social post | second | direct, one idea | one | threads of hype, emoji, manufactured urgency |
| Spotlight | third about the person | human and genuine, warm | one, soft | corporate gloss over a real person |
| Email | second | brief, one job per email | one primary; secondary links fine in a newsletter | subject-line hype, false deadlines |
| Onesheet | second | benefit-led, scannable | one, closing | feature lists with no outcome, unsupported claims |
| Video script | second | spoken, short lines, plain | one, closing | written-register sentences nobody says aloud |
| Video publishing kit | second | SEO-literal, keyword front-loaded | one, description | clickbait titles, keyword stuffing |

## Red flags

Signals a draft has drifted, even when nothing is technically broken and Vale is clean.

- The first sentence would work for any company in any industry.
- The word "solutions" appears and cannot be replaced with the actual thing.
- A paragraph makes a claim and the next one does not support it with a number, a name, or a step.
- Facilitron is the subject of more than a couple of sentences in a toolkit item.
- More than one CTA, or a CTA before the reader has been given anything.
- A checklist item that cannot be done. "Ensure optimal performance" is not a task; "Replace or clean
  air filters" is.
- An H2 that is a pun. Headings are read as search results and as a table of contents at once.
- The draft explains what a work order is to an audience that writes forty a week.
- It reads well out loud and you cannot say what it claims.
- Rhythm tells: "It's not just X, it's Y." "Here's the thing." Tricolons that build to nothing.

## Signature phrases

House language. Use deliberately; do not wear it out.

- "No subsidization without deliberation."
- "Public assets need public accountability."
- "Visible, intentional, and authorized."
- "Governed as carefully as they are maintained."
- "A public standard, not a proprietary product."
- "Use this checklist to..." and "Before you begin..." for toolkit openers.

## Using this file

Follow the spirit over the letter. The dash rule and the Vale pack are mechanical and get checked
mechanically. Everything here is a description of judgment, and judgment shifts with format: a press
release is more formal than a toolkit item, an email is shorter than both, a social post has room for
one idea.

Do not overfit. If a draft is technically compliant and reads like it was assembled from this file,
it is off-voice, because the corpus does not read that way. The test is not "did I follow the rules,"
it is "would this sit unremarkably next to the most recent thing we published."
